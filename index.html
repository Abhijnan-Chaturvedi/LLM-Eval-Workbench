LLM Evaluation Workbench

Compare models on your own task. A golden dataset check and an LLM judge score quality, then quality is weighed against cost and speed to give one recommendation you can defend.

The problem

Teams pick an LLM from a demo and a public leaderboard. Neither reflects their own data, and quality, cost and speed get decided by different people.

The approach

Run every model on the same test cases. Stage 1 checks each answer against the expected one. Stage 2 has a judge model score it. Disagreements go to a person.

Who it is for

Product managers choosing a model, engineers checking a prompt or model change, and business owners who need cost at a given quality.

Set up a live run

Works with any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Your key is sent only to the address you enter below. It is kept in this page's memory and is gone when you close the tab.

On OpenRouter, prices are looked up for you. On other endpoints, add input and output price per 1M tokens after the name, separated by |.

Each case costs one call per model plus one judge call per answer, billed to your key.

How the scores are calculated

  • Stage 1, golden dataset check. For classification, the answer must match the expected label. For free text, it is the word overlap (F1) with the expected answer; 60 or more counts as a pass. It is cheap and repeatable, but it cannot tell a correct paraphrase from a wrong one, or spot one wrong number in a long answer.
  • Stage 2, LLM judge. A judge model scores correctness, completeness and faithfulness from 1 to 5 and gives a reason. An answer passes when every dimension is 4 or 5.
  • Quality score. A blend of the two stages: 50/50 for classification, 30/70 for free text, where overlap is the weaker signal.
  • Stage agreement. The share of answers where both stages give the same pass or fail. Disagreements go to the review queue.
  • Cost per 1,000 requests. Input and output tokens multiplied by the model's price. Judge calls are not included in a model's cost.
  • Decision score. Your weights applied to quality, cost relative to the cheapest model, and speed relative to the fastest. Only models at or above the minimum quality can be recommended.
